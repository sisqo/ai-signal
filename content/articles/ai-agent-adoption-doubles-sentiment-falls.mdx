In 2025, 31% of developers used AI agents. In April 2026, 59% did. Both numbers come from [Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/09/30/getting-ready-for-2026-results-a-look-back-on-developer-survey-findings), the large question-and-answer site for programmers. Use nearly doubled in about one year. But developers' feelings about AI tools moved the other way.

This matters if you lead a team or pick tools for one. More use does not mean more trust. And the tool your team uses today may not be the tool it uses next year.

## What counts as an agent

An AI agent is more than a chat window. You give it a task, and it takes several steps on its own. It can read files, run commands, change code, and check its own work. Claude Code, Codex, and GitHub Copilot's agent mode are examples.

An older AI assistant only suggests code. You still copy it, run it, and check it. An agent does more of that work for you. So it also has more ways to go wrong.

## The use numbers

Stack Overflow runs a large survey of developers every year. Its 2025 survey found that 31% of developers used AI agents. In April 2026, it ran a smaller survey, called a pulse survey. That survey found 59%.

AI tools in general grew more slowly, because most developers already used them. The share of developers using any AI tool went from 44% in 2023 to 62% in 2024. In 2025, it reached 79%.

## The feeling numbers

Stack Overflow also asks developers how they feel about AI tools. Here the line goes down.

In 2024, 72% of developers felt positive about AI tools. In 2025, 59.7% did. Negative feelings rose from 6.4% to 20.4%. That is more than three times as many developers with a bad view of AI tools.

Students and other people still learning to code lost the most. Their positive feelings fell from 72.2% to 52.8%. Students' trust in AI answers fell too. In 2024, 49.1% of students trusted AI output at least moderately. In 2025, 37.4% did.

These feeling numbers are from 2025. On September 30, Stack Overflow said its 2026 results would come out within days. Those results will show if the trend continued.

## Why use and feeling can move apart

This is not a contradiction. Developers use more AI because it saves time on some tasks. They like it less because they now know where it fails.

We saw the same pattern in July, in [our article on what developers hand off to AI](/articles/what-developers-delegate-to-ai-2026). Developers give AI the easy, low-risk tasks. They keep the risky ones for themselves. One study even found developers felt faster with AI but were measured as slower. We covered it in [our article on that gap](/articles/developers-feel-faster-measured-slower).

An agent makes this sharper. It does more steps without you. When it is right, it saves more time. When it is wrong, the mistake is deeper inside the work, and it takes longer to find.

## The tools are changing fast

A second study shows a different kind of change. [JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2026/08/ai-coding-agent-adoption-2026/), the company that makes IntelliJ and other code editors, asked more than 15,000 developers about their tools. It ran the survey from May to July 2026.

| Tool | January 2026 | May–July 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Claude Code | 18% | 39% |
| GitHub Copilot | 29% a year earlier | 21% |
| Codex | 3% | 16% |
| Cursor | 18% | 12% |

These numbers show the share of developers who use each tool at work. Many people use more than one tool, so the numbers do not add up to 100%.

Claude Code more than doubled in six months. In the United States, 47% of developers use it. And 31% of all developers now call it their main tool. Codex grew more than five times, from 3% to 16%.

Two tools lost users. GitHub Copilot fell from 29% a year earlier to 21%. Cursor fell from 18% to 12%. The biggest drop was in China. There, Cursor fell from 28% in January to 16%.

JetBrains makes its own AI tools, so it is not a neutral observer. But its survey is large, and Stack Overflow saw the same direction. In its April pulse survey, Claude Code use among AI tool users rose from 41% to 55%.

## What this means for a team

Two lessons come out of these numbers.

First, switching is normal now. A developer who used Cursor in January may use Claude Code in July. A team that signs a two-year contract for one tool takes a real risk. Claude Code alone went from 18% to 39% in six months.

Second, more use brings more review work. An agent writes more code with less help from you. Someone still has to read that code. This may be one reason feelings fall while use rises. People do that checking work, and they find problems.

## What to do now

**Do not sign long contracts for AI coding tools.** Prefer monthly or yearly plans. The leading tool changed this year, and it can change again.

**Ask your team how they feel, not only what they use.** Run a short survey of your own. Ask which tasks the agent helps with and which tasks it makes slower. The answers tell you where to use it.

**Keep the risky tasks for people.** Let agents do tests, small fixes, and repeated changes. Keep a person in charge of deploys, security, and design choices.

**Read the Stack Overflow 2026 results when they appear.** They will show if developers' feelings kept falling in 2026. Or they may show feelings rising again as the tools improved.
